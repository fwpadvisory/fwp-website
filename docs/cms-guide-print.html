Adding and editing
articles on your website

A short guide to Sanity Studio — no technical knowledge needed.

Your website articles live in a content editor called Sanity Studio. You can write, edit and publish articles yourself — no developer needed, and nothing in the website code has to change.

1. Opening the editor

fwpadvisory.com.au/studio

While the site is still on its temporary address, use fwp-website-mu.vercel.app/studio.

Sign in with the Google account you used to create the Sanity project. You only need to do this once per device.

2. Writing a new article

  1. In the left-hand list, click Article.
  2. Click the + (new document) button at the top.
  3. Fill in the fields (see the next section).
  4. Click Publish (bottom right) when you are ready for it to go live.

Your article appears at the top of Latest Articles on the Resources page — newest first, automatically. You never need to sort or categorise anything.

3. What each field does

FieldWhat to put in it
TitleThe article headline.
SlugThe web address. Click Generate and it fills itself in from the title.
CategoryOptional. Leave blank if you like — it isn't shown on the website.
Excerpt / summary1–2 sentences. This is the text shown on the article card.
AuthorOptional. Leave blank and it shows the standard byline.
BodyThe article itself. See “Formatting” below.
Featured imageThe picture on the card and at the top of the article.
DisclaimerYour general-information disclaimer, shown in a box at the foot of the article.
Source noteOptional. Case references or sources, in small text at the very bottom.
SEO title / descriptionOptional. Only fill in if you want Google to show something different from the title and summary.
Publication dateThe date shown on the article and used to order the list.

4. Formatting the body

The Body field works like a simple word processor.

  • Type normally for ordinary paragraphs.
  • For a heading, highlight the line and choose H2 from the style dropdown (the box that says “Normal”).
  • For a bullet list, click the bullet-list icon in the toolbar.
  • For bold or italic, highlight the text and use the toolbar buttons.
Keep headings as H2 — the website styles them to match the rest of the site.

5. Adding a featured image

  1. Scroll to Featured image.
  2. Click Upload and choose a picture from your computer.
  3. After it uploads, drag the small circle on the image to choose which part stays visible when the picture is cropped on smaller screens.

Landscape pictures work best. Anything roughly 1200–1600 pixels wide is plenty.

6. Drafts, editing and unpublishing

  • Save a draft — just stop typing. Sanity saves automatically, and the document stays a draft until you press Publish. Drafts are never visible on the website.
  • Edit a published article — open it, make your changes, then press Publish again to push the update live.
  • Take an article down — open it, click the ⋮ menu at the bottom and choose Unpublish. It disappears from the website but stays in the editor.
  • Undo — the ⋮ menu also has document history, so you can return to an earlier version.

7. When do changes appear on the website?

The website is a fast static site, which means it is rebuilt after content changes rather than reading the database on every visit.

  • Right now: after you press Publish, let us know and we will refresh the site.
  • Once automatic publishing is switched on (waiting on one outstanding technical item), your changes will appear on their own within a few minutes.
If you have published something and don't see it on the site yet, that is why — the content is safe, the site just needs its refresh.

8. A few tips

  • Write in the editor rather than pasting from Word — pasted text sometimes brings hidden formatting with it. If you do paste, use Paste and match style (Cmd+Shift+V).
  • Always fill in the Excerpt — it is what people read on the card before clicking.
  • Keep the Disclaimer on every article for consistency.
  • Nothing you do in the editor can break the website design. The worst case is an article that needs tidying up.
